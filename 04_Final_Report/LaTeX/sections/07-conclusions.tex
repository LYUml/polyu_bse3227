\reportheading{7. Design assessment}
\subhead{7.1 Air distribution}
The 8-inch neck fits the 600 mm ceiling module and maintains throw at the 40\% VAV setting. At peak flow the facing jets from SD-77 and SD-78 have the smallest margin: their catalogue throw of 5.151 m gives a ratio just above 3.4 at the 1.500 m midplane. At minimum flow the limiting outlet shifts to SD-60, where the southward ratio remains above 1.0. These conclusions use catalogue isothermal throw and the load range discussed in Section 4.

\subhead{7.2 Ductwork and ceiling coordination}
The common trunk carries 13.09 m³/s through a 2200 × 600 mm section. The resulting 9.918 m/s leaves little margin below the 10 m/s main-duct limit. The branch with the largest Darcy friction gradient is D-08; Table 5 identifies it separately from the trunk that governs velocity.

The 600 mm nominal duct depth sets the space required by the bare duct. Installation also requires clearance for insulation, flanges and supports where the trunk passes beams or other services. The longest flexible connection allows 0.50 m beyond its plan length for routing. A coordinated ceiling section would establish the available clearance and installed drop.

\subhead{7.3 Implications for detailed design}
The most sensitive ceiling locations are the common trunk and the wing outlets, for different reasons. At A-01 the calculated speed is already close to the 10 m/s limit. A local reduction in section to clear another service would change both velocity and friction; the coordinated detail should preserve the required area or be checked at the full 13.09 m³/s.

At SD-77 and SD-78, the critical dimension is the spacing between centres. A change to either tile affects the facing throw ratio and the distances in the other discharge directions. The 8-inch selection also depends on the minimum settings at the boxes. For this reason the individual outlet flows in Appendix A, together with the group totals in Appendix C, are the appropriate reference values for balancing.

The straight-duct gradients in Table 5 identify where friction per metre is greatest. Selecting the fan would additionally require the lengths and fitting losses along each complete path, together with the pressure drops through the AHU and terminal equipment. The section with the highest gradient alone does not identify that path.
