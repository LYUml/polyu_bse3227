1. Introduction and design basis
1.1 Design objective
The design provides a variable air volume supply-air layout for the given open-plan office.
It covers the preliminary cooling load, maximum and minimum diffuser flows, classroom
air-diffusion checks, VAV connections and rectangular duct sizing required by the
assignment.
1.2 Adopted office and system assumptions
The supplied plan annotation of 1022 m2 is used for the rule-of-thumb load calculation.
The interpreted CAD boundary is used for diffuser locations and directional lengths. These
are different quantities: the annotated area is not presented as a surveyed net area. The
adopted layout has 80 diffusers, twenty VAV groups and 47 rigid supply sections.

1.3 Calculation scope
The empirical allowance and routing allowance are preliminary design assumptions. This
report distinguishes the classroom ratio check from a measured ADPI percentage. Full fan
selection and detailed system pressure are supplementary engineering studies, not part of
the assignment duct-sizing calculation.

2.2 Diffuser distribution and VAV grouping
Each diffuser receives its assigned share of the total airflow. The 80 maximum flows sum
to 12000.373 L/s, and every minimum flow is exactly 40% of its maximum. Each of the
twenty VAV groups serves four diffusers, satisfying the assignment requirement for four to
five outlets per box. Group flows equal the sum of their diffuser flows; the complete
grouping is given in Appendix C.
Twelve diffusers are displaced 150 mm south within their original explicit service areas.
Service-area loads, all terminal flows, VAV membership and rigid section sizes remain
unchanged; Figure 2 identifies the adjusted row.
2.3 Flexible connection design
The longest straight XY connection reconstructed from the saved/reopened drawing is
2.873 m. Adding the stated 0.50 m routing allowance gives 3.373 m. This leaves a
minimum conditional margin of 0.627 m to the adopted 4 m design cap, which is stricter
than the assignment limit of 5 m. The allowance is not a measured vertical offset or
installed slack.
Flexible ducts are specified as final connections, not main distribution. Final routing must
remain within the ordinary limit and must not pass through fire-resisting barriers or attach
directly to fire/fire-smoke dampers [1]. The actual installed lengths and fire-boundary
classification would be confirmed before installation.

3. Diffuser selection and air-diffusion checks
3.1 Selected diffuser and characteristic length
The selected Krueger 1400/51400 has a 600 x 600 mm panel, 8-inch neck and no damper
[2]. Both operating flows of all 80 diffusers lie within its published table. T0.25 is taken
from the third horizontal isothermal throw value; intermediate flows use linear
interpolation between adjacent published rows.
Characteristic length L is taken in each principal plan direction to a boundary or the
midplane between opposing outlets, as an explicit assumption of the planar design. The 320
directional lengths comprise 40 boundary-limited and 280 opposing-outlet cases. Appendix
B records all four lengths for every diffuser.
3.2 Maximum and minimum operating cases
All 640 directional operating cases lie within the adopted classroom interval T0.25/L =
1.0-3.4. Original catalogue interpolation gives 1.1526-3.0598; the independent native
K-Select check gives 1.2117-3.1122 [3]. Figure 1 shows the directional extremes for each
diffuser using the original table.

3.3 Interpretation and design approximation
The preliminary sensible allowance is 170.1 W/m2, 6.3% above the printed classroom table
limit of 160 W/m2. Using the classroom interval at this allowance is an explicit coursework
design approximation. It is not a teacher-issued exemption, a prediction of ADPI >=80%,
or a transfer of the manufacturer Type 3 curve [4]. No measured ADPI percentage is
claimed.

4. Duct sizing and Hong Kong design provisions
4.1 Rigid duct arrangement and sizing
The single-line plan connects the VAV groups to a branched supply trunk using straight
sections where practical. Each of the 47 sections is identified on the drawing and in
Appendix D. Rectangular area determines actual air velocity; the adopted main/branch
speed limits are 10/8 m/s. The upper friction criterion is 1.23 Pa/m and the preferred
aspect-ratio limit is 4:1.
All 47 adopted sections were checked in the assignment-linked Online Ductulator using 36
unique input combinations [5]. D06, D13 and D20 use 550 x 350 mm. The schedule retains
both the online friction value and an independent equivalent-round calculation; the two are
not assumed numerically identical. Section friction is a pressure gradient, not total fitting,
terminal or fan pressure.

5. Design assessment and conclusion
5.1 Consistency of the adopted design
The report, diffuser schedule, VAV grouping and duct schedule refer to one 80-diffuser
design. The CAD was saved and reopened in AutoCAD; generated geometry and all 80
straight connection lengths were checked against the calculation sources. The reviewed A1
plot uses a 1:100 scale, with a 100 mm reference measured at 99.991 mm. The formal
drawing is included after the appendices.
5.2 Independent calculation checks
Native K-Select GRD reports cover all 160 terminal operating flows through 49 distinct
whole-CFM inputs. Inputs differ from the prescribed flow by no more than 0.2331 L/s.
Independent PDF-coordinate checks reconcile the reported conditions and results. The
source imperial throw units are converted to metres; a low-flow NC dash remains qualified
unknown. These checks support numerical consistency, not installed performance
certification.
5.3 Conclusion and limitations
The adopted design provides 12000.373 L/s maximum and 4800.149 L/s minimum supply
air through twenty four-outlet VAV groups. All classroom directional ratio checks meet the
selected interval under the stated load-range approximation. The 47 rigid duct sections
meet the adopted sizing criteria, and the conditional flexible-connection budget is below
the stricter 4 m design cap.
The result is a preliminary coursework design. Its principal limitations are the empirical
cooling allowance, table-range approximation, interpreted plan boundaries and routing
allowance. Actual ceiling conditions, final installed lengths and product installation would
require confirmation before construction. Full-system pressure and fan selection remain
separate engineering work; supplementary linear-slot and CFD studies do not replace the
specified 1400/51400 coursework design.

Appendix E. Supplementary load-basis comparison
E.1 Purpose and status
The main coursework design, drawings and schedules retain the 189 W/m2 classroom
allowance, 0.90 sensible factor and specified 1400/51400 family. The assignment requests
a rule-of-thumb load; the classroom guide offers 189 W/m2 as a preliminary reference. This
appendix examines another explicit assumption. It does not replace the main design or
remove its ADPI applicability limitation.
A Hong Kong office study reports a whole-floor cooling load of 129 W/m2, excluding
safety/standby allowance [7]. Applying a declared 15% design margin gives 148.35 W/m2,
rounded upward to 150 W/m2. This is a coursework scenario, not a measured load for this
building. The study concerns one office floor with 1999 measurements; its 2001
publication does not establish a universal or current design allowance.
